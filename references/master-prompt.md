# The Master Brief and the Thinking Framework

This is the build's first input. Fill it in Phase 1, get it approved, then feed
it to the coding agent as ONE prompt. It merges the client intake, the design
system, and the conversion psychology into a single decision document. An
empty field is a decision delayed, not a decision avoided.

## How to use it

1. Interview the client (or yourself, for the TALINBUILDS brand) and fill
   every field. Fields 2 and 7 may only contain facts the client gave you.
2. Read it out loud in one pass. If any line sounds like a template, rewrite
   it in the client's own words.
3. Paste the filled brief to the coding agent as the single opening prompt of
   the build. The agent never invents brand facts, palette, or claims beyond
   what is written here.

## The Master Brief (fill every field)

ROLE: You are a senior product designer and engineer building a real website
for a real business. No lorem ipsum, no placeholder text, no template look,
no invented statistics or testimonials. Every visible word must be true to
this brief.

1. BUSINESS: [name], a [what it does] in [city] for [target customer]
   who want [the outcome they actually want, in their words].

2. TRUTH (only what the client confirmed): years open [x], customers
   served [x], qualifications [x], real testimonials [paste exact text
   or leave out]. Never invent a number, review, or award.

3. MOOD: [aesthetic + a real-world reference, e.g. "the inside of a
   high-end watchmaker's workshop at night"]. Mode: [dark / light].
   Clinics, schools, and anything trust-first: light mode.

4. COLOR, 60/30/10, use exactly these and nothing else:
   Primary 60%: [#hex + real-world reference, backgrounds and surfaces]
   Secondary 30%: [#hex, cards, nav, dividers only]
   Accent 10%: [#hex + reference, CTAs, active states, key callouts ONLY,
   never headings or body copy]
   Never pure black or pure white as canvas. All text must pass contrast.

5. TYPE: [display font] for headings at [weight], minimum H1 [64-72px],
   [body font] for body and UI, [mono font] for small labels. Never
   Inter, Roboto, or Arial as display. Body line height 1.7 to 1.8,
   max line length 65 to 70 characters.

6. SPACE: section padding at least 120px top and bottom, at least 80px
   between content blocks. If anything looks crowded, add space, never less.

7. HERO, all five parts:
   a. Outcome headline in the customer's words, not a feature list
      (write it here): [headline]
   b. Subheadline with one specific detail or result in the first
      [timeframe]: [subheadline]
   c. ONE CTA button reading "[label]", nothing competing with it
   d. Background: [describe the exact image or footage]
   e. Trust signal: [customer count or one real quote, from field 2]

8. LAYOUT: [Bento Grid / Split-Screen / Storytelling] structure.
   Sections, each with one job, funnelling to the single CTA:
   [section 1: name + its job]
   [section 2: ...]
   [section 3: ...]
   Final CTA: [WhatsApp deep link / call / form / checkout].

9. MOTION: scroll-eased entrances, hover feedback, smooth transitions,
   nothing bouncing or spinning. One subtle interactive moment per
   section. Honor prefers-reduced-motion.

10. PSYCHOLOGY, mark where each is used:
    Smart defaults in every form field. Progress never starts at zero.
    Give value before asking for anything (reciprocity). Where honest,
    frame what the visitor loses by not acting, not only what they gain.
    Anchor prices with contrast, never show a cost in isolation.

11. VOICE: [brand register, one line]. Buyers' own phrases to use:
    [pain phrase], [outcome phrase], [objection to answer].
    Banned words: leverage, seamless, empower, unlock, robust,
    actionable, data-driven, solutions, testament, landscape, delve,
    elevate. No em dashes. No "it's not just X, it's Y" constructions.

12. GUARDS: one brand name used end to end. Every image, logo, and
    testimonial belongs to THIS business. Footer discloses if the
    brand is fictional. Read all visible words end to end before
    declaring done.

## The thinking framework (the agent's self-review, run at every gate)

1. The Halo check (first 50 milliseconds). Screenshot the very first
   viewport. Ask: would a stranger trust this business within two
   seconds? One committed visual direction, generous space, one clear
   message. If the screenshot could belong to any business, it fails.
2. The Load check (cognitive fluency). Fresh eyes for five seconds,
   then three questions: what does this business do, who is it for,
   what do I do next. Any hesitation means remove something. Every
   section gets one primary goal.
3. The Delight check (peak-end rule). Name three micro-moments a
   visitor will remember: a hover response, a load transition, a
   scroll reveal. If the count is zero, add one per section at
   whisper level.
4. The Truth check. Every number, review, and claim traces back to
   field 2 of the brief. Anything untraceable comes off the page.
5. The Coherence check. Read every visible word end to end. One brand
   name throughout. No orphaned copy, logo, or testimonial from a
   different business hiding in a section built from a reference image.
